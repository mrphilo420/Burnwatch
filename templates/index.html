      Method

      How it works

      1 · Calibrate

      Exchangeable humans

      Human-written documents are scored once, offline, under the chosen generative model. Development statistics fix every transformation before calibration begins.

      2 · Rank

      Conservative conformal p

      Each score gets p = (1 + #{calibration ≥ test}) / (m + 1): the tie-conservative upper-tail rank. No independence among tokens is assumed.

      3 · Control

      Pathwise guarantee

      A: a union bound over a registered family permits score-dependent action selection. B: a calibrated complete-path maximum permits early stopping without splitting the budget.

      The inspection route

      A policy reads the first min(b, L) tokens of the document, chooses a detector at each budget, and stops at a valid alert, a development-fixed futility rule, or the resource cap.

      Current route: likelihood @ 128 → likelihood @ 256 → rank @ 512 → log-rank @ 1,024. Detector family: likelihood, rank, log-rank, and the stop-slop lexicon (plus a Binoculars-style contrasting-model score and Fast-DetectGPT conditional curvature when enabled). DetectGPT T5-perturbation curvature remains a planned addition per the proposal's operational protocol; policies may skip registered actions.

      The two constructions

      Construction A: allocate error across actions

      Weighted Bonferroni testing over a registered family of prefix–detector scores Sa. Each action gets its own error share; a union bound protects whichever subset is executed.

      pa(X) = (1 + Σi 1{Sa(Hi) ≥ Sa(X)}) / (m+1)   ·   alert if pa(X) ≤ α·wa

      wa are fixed before calibration with Σ wa ≤ 1. Choice of executed actions may use observed scores or ranks; the family and weights are frozen.

      Construction B: calibrate the complete path

      One calibrated scalar instead of an allocation: the maximum of the development-fixed route, Mπ(X). Partial-path maxima are always ≤ the complete maximum, so stopping early is free.

      Mπ(X) = maxt gat{Sat(X)}   ·   alert if ptpath(X) ≤ α

      Failures are sentineled: Sa = −∞ and ga(−∞) = −∞, so a failed action adds no alert evidence. Complete calibration routes are computed with alert-based stopping disabled.

      Calibration counts

      The smallest deterministic conformal rank is 1/(m+1). These counts permit rejection on the rank grid: useful detection power may require more data.

      Independent audit

      With zero false alerts in N0 independent human documents, the one-sided 95% upper confidence limit on the deployment false-alert probability is U0.95 = 1 − 0.051/N₀ (Clopper–Pearson).

      …

      For U0.95 < 0.001 the requirement is N0 ≥ 2,995 zero-error observations. The 95% statement concerns coverage over repeated independent audits.

      Limits and responsible use

      The guarantee is real, but it is bounded. These four simulations show exactly where it stops holding.

      1 · Distribution shift

      The guarantee holds for the calibration population. A new human population with distance δ shifts the bound to α + TV(P₀, P̃₀), and the empirical FPR follows it.

      shift δ …

      2 · Subgroups

      A pooled marginal bound does not protect a shifted subgroup. Drag the subgroup's shift and its share of the test stream: the subgroup FPR clears α while the pooled FPR stays near it.

      3 · Repeated deployment

      One-document control does not cover a collection. Screening K independent documents at level α gives a false-alert probability of 1 − (1 − α)^K across the set.

      A conformal rank is a p-value against the human-reference null, not a posterior probability of AI authorship. Drag the score: the two curves diverge.

      About this tool

      Two conformal constructions for adaptive within-document screening. Construction A registers a finite prefix–detector family, allocates α across weighted conformal ranks, and protects any executed subset by union bound. Construction B calibrates the complete-path maximum of a development-fixed route, so partial-path maxima are bounded by the calibrated maximum and early stopping is free. Both control the marginal probability of any false alert at level α under document-level exchangeability. Proposal stage: the empirical power and cost study remains to be conducted; this interface is the proof of concept.

      DetectGPT · Mitchell, Lee, Khazatsky, Manning & Finn, Stanford CRFM, 2023 · Binoculars · Hans, Schwarzschild, Cherepanova, Kazemi, Saha, Goldblum, Geiping & Goldstein, UMD, ICML 2024 (evaluation corpora).

      FAQ

      Frequently asked questions

      It screens one document against a specified human-reference population using two conformal constructions over prefix–detector scores. The output is a decision: flag for review or no alert at this budget, with a provable marginal bound on any false alert: P(any alert under the null) ≤ α.
      The proposal's analytic results are implemented as a working system: the calibration pipeline (development/calibration split, frozen transformations, futility rule), both constructions, and an empirical benchmark over the Binoculars paper corpora. Verified behaviors: human alert rates track α under same-domain calibration; AI-generated documents produce lower conformal p-values and alert at the promised level. What remains is the proposal's evaluation study: matched-power comparisons against fixed policies and the cost profiling of the six-month plan. This interface is the executable proof of concept of the theory.
      A flag for review when the document is human-written relative to the reference population. The guarantee is marginal over calibration data, the new null document, and policy randomness: it does not certify any particular calibration set or subgroup. An alert concerns departure from the human reference; it does not by itself establish AI authorship or misconduct.
      Fixed thresholds are tuned to specific models and datasets and carry no finite-sample guarantee. A conformal rank adapts the boundary to the calibration sample and gives a finite-sample guarantee under exchangeability, with no assumption of token independence. Detector families are interchangeable: a Binoculars-style contrasting score is one of the available detectors, and DetectGPT-class curvature scores could enter the same registered family.
      Weighted Bonferroni testing over a registered family of prefix–detector actions. Each action a gets a conformal p-value pa = (1 + #{calibration ≥ test})/(m+1) and its own error allocation α·wa with fixed weights summing to ≤ 1. Alert if any executed action satisfies pa ≤ α·wa. The union bound protects score-dependent selection within the family. Default allocations are resolution-aware: the budget is concentrated on the largest equal-weight active subset the rank grid can reject at the current m (route actions first), so Construction A fulfills its rejection requirement instead of splitting α into unreachable shares. Full-family equal weights (16 actions) would need m ≥ 319 at α=0.05 and m ≥ 1,599 at α=0.01.
      Calibration of one scalar: the complete-path maximum Mπ(X) of a development-fixed route. Since every partial-path maximum is ≤ the complete maximum, the terminal conformal rank protects early stopping without splitting the error budget. Alert at the first executed action with ptpath ≤ α. Needs only m ≥ 99 at α = 0.01, far fewer calibration documents than A.
      They are rank-feasibility minima: the smallest conformal rank is 1/(m+1), so rejection requires m ≥ ⌈1/(α·wa)⌉ − 1 for A and m ≥ ⌈1/α⌉ − 1 for B. Meeting the count permits rejection on the rank grid; it does not guarantee useful detection power: a score must also actually attain the minimum rank. The counts table in "How it Works" shows whether the current calibration m satisfies them.
      With zero false alerts in N0 independent human audit documents, the one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson upper limit is U = 1 − 0.051/N₀. For U < 0.001 you need at least 2,995 zero-error observations. This is a fixed-sample audit statement: outcome-dependent stopping needs a sequential confidence procedure.
      A token budget is not a sample size. The exchangeable units are documents; tokens within a document are dependent, and the constructions explicitly allow arbitrary dependence. Treating prefixes of one document as separate calibration units would require a different exchangeability argument. Oracle sample-complexity bounds (e.g., the Hoeffding budget of the proposal) are illustrations for independent scores, not token-count guarantees.
      The conformal p-value is a single number for the document; the decision rule is p ≤ α. The same p-value crosses a looser bound and clears a stricter one: that is calibration working as intended, and why the interface always shows the p-value alongside the bound.
      Calibration and evaluation follow the proposal's data design: separate development, calibration, and held-out evaluation. Human calibration documents come from IMDB reviews, the Binoculars paper corpora (cc_news / cnn / pubmed), or the deck-recommended corpora: RealDet (Zhu et al., ACL 2025; 15 domains, bilingual, our default calibration corpus), RAID (Dugan et al., ACL 2024; clean rows, attack = none), and DetectRL-X (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2024; English paired rows). All three are downloaded and cached locally on first use, in eligible English subsets. Duplicates and paraphrase lineages should be grouped before splitting; the benchmark offsets past calibration documents so development/calibration/evaluation never overlap on the same corpus.
      Run the server with the recommended calibration corpus: python app.py --corpus realdet (RAID and DetectRL-X are equally valid; the MCP paper found RealDet calibration the most stable across test sets). Use the benchmark panel to evaluate on any corpus: the deck's matched-error comparators and the ≥ 20% cost criterion are reported directly in the cost column.
      The scoring model is selectable in the Screen tab: gpt2, gpt2-medium, gpt2-large, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Pythia-1.4B, or SmolLM2-1.7B, each with its own cached calibration (first use builds it, ~1–4 minutes). The detector family is likelihood, rank, and log-rank, with an optional Binoculars-style contrasting-model detector (observer vs. performer) and an optional Fast-DetectGPT conditional-curvature detector (reference/scoring pair, analytic discrepancy). The constructions are detector-agnostic; DetectGPT T5-perturbation curvature remains a planned addition for the evaluation study. Human calibration corpora: IMDB reviews or the Binoculars cc_news / cnn / pubmed news corpora.
      Likelihood-based scores under a small model treat frontier-model prose as nearly human: GPT-2-class scores give Gemini-style text p-values around 0.3–0.9. The conformal constructions guarantee false-alert control, not detection of every generator; power is precisely what the proposal's evaluation study must measure. In practice, the strongest available configuration for frontier text is the contrasting-model detector (gpt2 observer vs. gpt2-medium performer) with Construction A: it drives Gemini-style text to p ≈ 0.015, near the α = 0.1 boundary, and stronger scoring models help modestly. If the bound holds at your chosen α and the text stays under it, the procedure reports insufficient evidence, which is the honest output for text the scores cannot separate.
      Two reasons, both visible in the data. Small samples scatter around α (n = 10–20). And cross-domain evaluation: calibrating on one corpus, testing on another, violates the null, turning the bound into α + TV(P₀, P̃₀), the distribution-shift bound of the proposal. Same-domain calibration (calibrate and benchmark on the same corpus) makes human rates track α.
      No. A conformal rank is a p-value against the human-reference null: it measures departure from the calibration population, not the posterior probability of AI authorship. That is why the interface reports flag for review and insufficient evidence rather than a percentage.
      Marginal conformal validity does not imply the conditional supermartingale property needed for betting-style sequential inference: e.g., U ~ Unif(0,1) is a valid p-value yet E(1/U) = ∞, and dependence between successive factors requires a separate argument. The theory deliberately constructs no e-process and justifies no products of transformed ranks: stopping control comes from the complete-path containment instead. See the paper appendix.
      By default an uploaded file is scored on its first scored prefix (the first 2,000 words, then the first 1,024 tokens the detectors read) — the view the calibration bundles are built for. The random contiguous window option instead picks a contiguous slice from anywhere in the full extracted document, using the seed you enter (or an auto-generated one). The result shows the seed and starting word so the same window can be reproduced exactly, and the audit record stores the sampling configuration. One caveat: the current conformal calibration is prefix-based, so random-window p-values are exploratory — they do not carry the nominal α guarantee until calibration is rebuilt with the same sampling policy.
